Dermatology Reference Guide

Common Skin Diseases (10 Clinical Types)

Know the signs • Get the right treatment • Keep your skin healthy.

Filter Type:
Sebaceous

Acne

Pimples, blackheads and whiteheads, usually on the face, chest and back.

Atopic

Eczema

Dry, itchy, inflamed skin that may be triggered by allergies or irritants.

Fungal

Fungal (Ringworm)

Itchy, circular, scaly patches that can spread through skin contact.

Autoimmune

Psoriasis

Red, raised patches covered with silvery scales, often on elbows, knees, and scalp.

Allergic

Urticaria (Hives)

Raised, itchy welts that appear suddenly and may come and go rapidly.

Parasitic

Scabies

Intense itching, especially at night, with small bumps or burrows between fingers.

Pigmentary

Vitiligo

White patches on the skin due to loss of skin colour cells (melanocytes).

Contact

Skin Allergy

Redness, itching, swelling or rash due to contact allergens or irritants.

Bacterial

Cellulitis

Red, painful, warm and swollen skin, often with fever. Urgent medical care.

Viral

Herpes (Cold Sores)

Painful fluid-filled blisters, usually on the lips or around mouth.

Common Symptoms

  • • Itching or irritation
  • • Redness and swelling
  • • Rashes or spots
  • • Dry, cracked or peeling skin
  • • Blisters or fluid-filled bumps
  • • Changes in skin colour
  • • Pain, sores or skin infections

Common Causes

1. Bacterial, viral or fungal infections

2. Allergies (foods, medicines, cosmetics)

3. Genetic and immune-system conditions

4. Poorly tolerated skin products or chemicals

5. Excessive sun exposure

6. Hormonal changes & underlying conditions

Prevention & Skin Care

• Wash gently with mild soap and lukewarm water.

• Use a suitable fragrance-free moisturiser.

• Apply broad-spectrum sunscreen daily.

• Avoid sharing towels or clothes if infection is suspected.

• Do not scratch affected skin to prevent secondary infection.

• Avoid steroid combination creams without medical advice.

What is Diabetes?

A chronic condition where your body cannot properly use glucose for energy because it doesn't produce enough insulin or can't use it well.

Insulin = Keys to use sugar as energy.

1. Type 1: No insulin produced (childhood/young age).

2. Type 2: Body becomes resistant to insulin (most common).

3. Gestational: Occurs during pregnancy.

Common Symptoms

Increased thirst Frequent urination Tiredness & weakness Increased hunger Blurred vision Slow healing wounds

Risks & Complications

• Family history & genetics

• Unhealthy diet & refined carbs

• Inactivity & sedentary habits

• Overweight / high BMI

• Age 45+ or high blood pressure

If Uncontrolled:
Heart disease Kidney damage Eye problems Nerve damage

How to Manage

✔ Eat Healthy: Whole grains, lean protein

✔ Stay Active: 30 mins brisk walk daily

✔ Avoid Sugary Drinks & sweets

✔ Manage stress & 7-8 hrs regular sleep

Respiratory Medicine & Infection Control

7 Types of Tuberculosis (T.B.)

Mycobacterium tuberculosis infection classifications with transmission dynamics and clinical manifestations (from public health protocols).

1. Pulmonary T.B. Airborne Spread

Affects the Lungs

Affects the lungs. It is the most common type and can spread to others through airborne droplets when coughing or sneezing.

Site: Lungs Contagious
2. Extrapulmonary T.B. Non-Spread

Outside the Lungs

Affects organs outside the lungs, such as the brain (meningitis), bones, kidneys, lymph nodes, intestines, or skin.

Site: Brain, Spine, Kidneys Non-Contagious
3. Latent T.B. Infection Dormant

Inactive Bacteria

The TB bacteria are present in the body but are inactive. The person usually has no symptoms and is not contagious.

Site: Body Tissues Can Reactivate
4. Active T.B. Disease Urgent

Symptomatic Illness

The bacteria multiply and cause active illness. It can be contagious, especially if it affects lungs or throat (cough, night sweats).

Site: Lungs / Systemic Requires DOTS
5. Miliary T.B. Disseminated

Bloodstream Spread

Caused by spread of TB bacteria through bloodstream. Leads to many tiny millet seed-like nodules across multiple organs.

Site: Multi-organ Severe
6. Drug-Resistant T.B. MDR / XDR

Medicine Resistant

Bacteria resistant to one or more primary TB medicines (Isoniazid, Rifampicin). Harder to treat, requiring second-line drugs.

Site: Systemic Resistance Govt Special Protocol
7. T.B. in Children Pediatric

Household Contact

Children get TB through close contact with infected adults. Symptoms can include low fever, chronic cough, and failure to gain weight.

Site: Pediatric / Lymph BCG Preventable
